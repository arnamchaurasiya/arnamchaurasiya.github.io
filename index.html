About Me

Why product management feels like the right home for how I think

I like sitting at the intersection of user pain, business goals, and execution reality. The work that energizes me most is making complexity understandable and making decisions easier for teams.

My Story

I started from engineering, but I kept moving closer to the questions behind the build: what problem is worth solving, what trade-offs matter, and what evidence should shape the roadmap. That pull led me from student-led execution and technical leadership into product analysis, user stories, sprint discussions, and product discovery work.

Today, I’m intentionally building toward product management because it lets me combine structured thinking, user empathy, analytical work, and collaborative execution in one role.

How I Work
  • I reduce ambiguity with scoped problems, sharper questions, and documented decisions.
  • I translate raw feedback into user stories, priorities, and execution-ready artifacts.
  • I balance user value, delivery constraints, and business context instead of optimizing for one in isolation.
  • I care about clarity because clear thinking is one of the highest-leverage product skills.
Product Philosophy

Start small, learn quickly, deepen intentionally.

I believe strong PM work is not about having the flashiest idea. It is about identifying the right problem, finding the smallest credible solution, agreeing on success metrics, and staying honest about what the data and users are actually saying.

Hackathon Winner Public sector | Product discovery | MVP scoping

HydroGuard

A government-facing irrigation monitoring platform built for Smart India Hackathon 2024 under the Ministry of Jal Shakti.

Problem

Reservoir and irrigation monitoring decisions were slowed by fragmented data, legacy processes, and limited visibility into siltation and climate-related risks.

Research / Insights

Through stakeholder conversations and domain feedback, I learned that teams needed one decision surface, not multiple technical tools competing for attention.

What I Did

Led discovery, prioritized the MVP, shaped the product story, and refined the backlog after every expert feedback round.

Methods

Stakeholder mapping, MVP scoping, backlog refinement, JTBD-style framing, pitch iteration.

Solution

Designed a focused platform concept around reservoir health, siltation tracking, climate impact assessment, and decision-ready monitoring views.

Impact / Learnings

Won Smart India Hackathon 2024 and secured INR 100,000. More importantly, I learned how powerful clear prioritization becomes when time is limited and stakeholders are diverse.

Internship Project B2B product analysis | Sprint planning

Product Usage Analysis at Goyalinfocom

Product analysis work across web and mobile modules to surface friction points and help teams move from vague asks to clearer sprint-ready scope.

Problem

Teams had multiple client and internal requirements coming in, but the path from product signal to prioritized work was not always clean.

Research / Insights

I analyzed usage data with Excel and SQL, paired that with requirement context, and identified recurring pain points across two product surfaces.

What I Did

Converted findings into structured user stories, supported sprint planning, and prepared documentation that improved clarity across product, engineering, and operations.

Methods

Trend analysis, user story writing, feature scoping, acceptance-criteria thinking, backlog prioritization.

Solution

Built a more actionable input layer for the team, connecting user pain points to better-defined feature scopes and delivery conversations.

Impact / Learnings

Identified three repeated friction themes and improved handoff quality. Estimated documentation structure reduced requirement clarification loops by about 30 percent in later planning discussions.

Student Platform Information architecture | Team leadership

GENE Society Website

A society website redesign and build effort where the core challenge was not only visual cleanup, but making information easier to find and easier to maintain over time.

Problem

Students needed announcements, event information, and team updates quickly, but content organization and navigation were not intuitive enough.

Research / Insights

I mapped key student journeys and found that discoverability mattered as much as design polish because most users came with a specific job to complete.

What I Did

Led a 15-member team, aligned design and development decisions, and helped refine UI components and navigation flows.

Methods

User journey mapping, information architecture, navigation simplification, modular content planning.

Solution

Created a clearer website structure with role-aware logic, modular content updates, and better pathing to high-intent content.

Impact / Learnings

Reduced key-content access from roughly four clicks to two in the revised structure and made long-term updates more scalable. I learned that governance and maintainability are product decisions too.

Workflow Design Edtech operations | Role-based systems

ICMP: Improvement Course Management Portal

A structured portal concept designed to manage multi-role academic workflows with clearer permissions, approvals, and status visibility.

Problem

Multi-step academic approvals often break down when responsibilities, permissions, and current status are unclear across stakeholders.

Research / Insights

The biggest risk was not screen complexity. It was decision ambiguity between Students, Coordinators, HODs, and DOAA.

What I Did

Defined role-based workflows, clarified access boundaries, and created reusable UI patterns in Figma to support consistency.

Methods

Workflow mapping, service blueprint thinking, state design, component systems, edge-case planning.

Solution

A portal structure with explicit role actions, approval checkpoints, and reusable interface patterns that made the process easier to understand at each step.

Impact / Learnings

Simplified a complex academic process into four primary user flows and made future design extension easier through reusable components. I learned how much product quality depends on system clarity behind the UI.
